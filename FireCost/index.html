The Cost of Fire, 2026

    How these numbers were made
    • Fires: wildfires in WFIGS discovered January 1 to November 1, 2026 that report an estimated cost above zero or reached 500 acres or more. Prescribed burns and complex parent records are excluded.
    • Cost: the WFIGS estimated cost to date, as entered by each incident. It covers fighting the fire, not property loss, recovery or economic damage. Many small fires never report a cost; they show as not reported and add nothing to the totals, so the totals are low.
    • Cost over time: each step shows the highest cost reported up to that date on the perimeter records. The last step, marked "Latest cost report," is the figure on the incident record when the data was extracted.
    • Final estimate: the incident's own projection of final cost, where one was entered.
    • Acres: the ranked list uses the incident's reported size. The time series uses the mapped perimeter acres, so the two can differ slightly.
    • Timing: perimeters are dated by the record's modified date, which can trail the actual mapping time. The animation keeps the last perimeter of each UTC day and drops records that shrink, since those are remaps or errors.
    • Active fire edge: the red edge marks where a perimeter differs from the one before it. Edges that did not move are drawn as a plain outline, and steps that only update cost show no red edge. Movement smaller than about 10 to 60 meters, depending on fire size, is not marked.
    • Fires not mapped: 749 fires have no perimeter on record. They appear in the lists, totals and U.S. map, with a map of the reported location only.
    • Fire start: the point of origin on the WFIGS incident record. A few points sit outside the first mapped perimeter, since both are reported separately.
    • Ignition cause: the FireCause field in WFIGS (Human, Natural or Undetermined), with the general and specific cause where an investigator entered one.
    • Structures, deaths and injuries: WFIGS does not carry structure or injury counts. Structures destroyed and damaged, and injuries, come from news and agency reports for 46 fires: the 25 costliest plus those listed in Wikipedia's 2026 United States wildfires table. Each fire's figure links to its source. Deaths use the larger of the WFIGS fatality count and the reported count for each fire. All of these totals are incomplete: a fire or state with no figure may still have had losses, and "at least" counts are added at their minimum.
    • Personnel: the largest number of people assigned at any report.
    • All fires map: the national map uses an Albers equal area projection, with Alaska and Hawaii inset. State maps use an equal area conic fitted to each state.
    • GIF: Create GIF saves the open fire's time series as an animated image, drawn flat and north-up with the layers you have switched on. Long fires are sampled to at most 60 frames.
    • Opening photo: the Kaiser Canyon Fire along Highway 155 in Washington, July 23, 2026, by the U.S. Forest Service via InciWeb (public domain), tinted in the Redstone GIS colors.
    • Playback: when a fire finishes playing, the map holds for five seconds and then opens and plays the next fire in the list, in whatever order and search the list is showing. Pause stops it.
    • Loading: each fire map stays covered until its base map, terrain, ownership, roads, rivers and county lines have loaded. If an outside map service is slow, the map is shown after 12 seconds and the late layer is added when it arrives.
    • Opening view: each fire opens in 3D on shaded relief with land ownership on, and the view slowly circles the fire until you touch or drag the map. The Rotate view switch turns the rotation off or back on.
    • 3D terrain: drapes the map over elevation from the Mapzen terrain tiles hosted by Amazon Web Services, with heights exaggerated 1.4 times so relief reads at fire scale. The PDF map is always flat and north-up.
    • Base maps: shaded relief and imagery load from USGS The National Map when you open a fire. Imagery is the USGS orthoimagery base map, which is built from NAIP aerial photography at close zooms. It mostly predates these fires, and detail softens past its closest level.
    • Land ownership: BLM Surface Management Agency, drawn from BLM's map tiles. Tribal and Alaska Native lands are grouped with other federal lands. Private and unknown land is left clear.
    • Ocean: drawn in the same blue as lakes for fires near a coast. The shoreline comes from Census TIGERweb water areas, and open water beyond them from elevation at or below sea level.
    • Roads, rivers and counties: interstates, U.S. highways, state routes, county roads and county lines from Census TIGERweb; rivers and lakes from the USGS National Hydrography Dataset. These load live, so they can be slow or briefly unavailable.
    • Sources: NIFC WFIGS Incident Locations and Daily Perimeters, extracted November 3, 2026. USGS The National Map. BLM Surface Management Agency. US Census TIGERweb and us-atlas. USGS NHD. Page type is Oswald and Public Sans; labels drawn on the map use Barlow.
