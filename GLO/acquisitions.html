About this map

Each area is land the United States took from another established nation by treaty, purchase, act of Congress or occupation. Outlines are from the National Atlas of the United States, "Territorial Acquisitions of the United States" (U.S. Geological Survey; a federal work in the public domain). Texas and West Florida are divided here along present state lines.

The map shows who had passed what to the United States by 31 December of the selected year. Each area is drawn from the date its instrument was signed or enacted, as on the state boundaries page; the dates it came into force and the land changed hands are in the panel. An area is marked gold for five years.

These instruments passed sovereignty and the government's claim between nations. They did not end Native title, which was taken separately by treaties with Native nations, and most of them promised to respect private grants already made by the earlier power. The notes on property are summaries that have not yet been checked against the treaty texts.

Acres are measured from the outlines. "BLM" figures are from the Bureau of Land Management's Public Land Statistics, Table 1-1, which counts only land that became federal public domain. The acres drawn for an instrument count every area it covered, including claims later given up.

Not drawn, because the source has no outline for them: the state cessions of western land (1781–1802); the Jay Treaty (1794); the Treaty of San Lorenzo (1795), which settled the 31st parallel; the Webster–Ashburton Treaty (1842), which settled the Maine boundary; later boundary awards; and Guam, American Samoa, the Northern Marianas and the Philippines. Treaty texts: Bevans, Treaties and Other International Agreements of the United States, 1776–1949 (Library of Congress).

Outlines are simplified to about 400 metres. Alaska is an inset at about a third of its true size; Hawaii, Puerto Rico and the Virgin Islands are insets at true size.
