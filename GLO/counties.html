About this map

Each outline is a dated version of a county from the Atlas of Historical County Boundaries, John H. Long, editor (Chicago: The Newberry Library, 2010): 17,727 county outlines and 220 state and territory outlines.

The map shows the outlines in force on 31 December of the selected year. A county is marked for five years after it was created, its boundary moved, or its state or territory changed. Land the atlas maps as a non-county area is drawn with a dashed line and left out of the counts. Blank land is not in the atlas for that year.

The atlas ends on 31 December 2000. Later changes follow the U.S. Census Bureau's list of substantial county changes, with outlines from its 2010 and 2024 boundary files: Broomfield County, Colorado (2001), two Virginia cities that rejoined their counties (2001, 2013), an exchange between York County and Newport News (2007), the Alaska borough and census area changes of 2007, 2008, 2013 and 2019, and two renamings in 2015. Smaller boundary adjustments after 2000 are not shown. Connecticut keeps its eight counties here, although since 2022 the Census Bureau reports by nine planning regions instead.

State lines begin in 1783, where the atlas's state and territory series begins. Outlines are simplified to about 500 metres for the web, so use the atlas files for close work. Alaska and Hawaii are drawn as insets, Alaska at about a third of its true size.
