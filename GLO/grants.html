What this shows

  • Acres of federal public land granted to each state, in nine purpose columns grouped here into seven. Hover a state for its breakdown; click it for its full list and its own chart.
  • Each grant is placed in the year of the act or of statehood, not the year the land changed hands. School sections passed as townships were surveyed, and swamp, railroad and quantity grants were selected and patented over decades. Alaska's 103 million acres appear in 1959 and were conveyed through the following half century.
  • The nineteen states with a dashed outline never held federal public land. Their grant was agricultural college scrip under the 1862 Morrill Act (Kentucky also had an 1826 asylum grant), which was sold and located on public land in other states, so no share of their own area is shown.
  • Most granted land was later sold by the states. This is what they were given, not what they hold today.

Sources and limits

  • Acreage: Bureau of Land Management, Public Land Statistics 1973, Table 4, "Grants to States, 1803–1973", read from scanned text and checked: every state row and every purpose column adds to the printed totals (328,424,871 acres) after eight single-figure reading corrections.
  • Years: each grant sits at its act or at statehood. The acts were checked on 21 Nov 2026 against the Statutes at Large, Gates's History of Public Land Law Development and the swamp-land regulations. Still by rule and not read act by act: West Virginia's scrip (1864), Nevada's and Colorado's college grants (1866, 1876), and the 1856 date for Michigan and Wisconsin railroads.
  • A state with several railroad, canal or road acts is shown at the first one. New Mexico's school grant is split in half between the 1898 territorial act and statehood.
  • The split of "other schools" between the 1862 college grant and earlier university grants uses 30,000 acres per member of Congress under the 1860 apportionment.
  • "Other purposes" mixes salt springs, public buildings, desert-land and reservoir grants; outside Alaska all of it is dated at statehood.
  • Grants made directly to railroad companies (about 94 million acres) are not grants to states and are not included. Hawaii had no federal public domain.
  • State outlines: U.S. Census Bureau cartographic boundary file, 2024. State areas are land area.
