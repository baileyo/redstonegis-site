Vermont proprietors' lots

52 towns, 9,280 lots, 2,069 of them with a name from the plan

Vermont's land was granted a town at a time. The grantees of each charter, the proprietors, then divided their town among themselves by drawing lots, usually in two or three divisions. The main map shows the charters. These pages go one step down, to the lots, for the towns where a regular plan of the division survives in the state's collection of town lotting plans.

How to read them. The lot lines are fitted, not surveyed. Each plan was read for its rows, columns and numbering, and the lots were laid out inside the present town outline, in most towns by dividing each block evenly. A line can be off by tens of metres, and by more where the town's boundary has moved since the lots were drawn. A name is whatever is written in that lot on the plan. On some plans that is the first proprietor; on others it is a later owner, as noted. Names were read once and spellings are as written.

Sixteen of the towns carry names. The rest show the lots and their numbers only, with public rights (minister, school, college, glebe) marked where the plan marks them.

Not included

Plans were found online for 203 Vermont towns. Sixty-five of those were regular enough to try, and 52 are here. Nine could not be built: Barnard, Chittenden and Peacham are uneven and would need tracing lot by lot; Brookfield and Plainfield survive only as faint copies; Sudbury, Coventry, Waitsfield and Lincoln have changed shape since they were lotted and cannot yet be tied to the present outline. Towns whose plans are irregular or in many separate blocks were not attempted.
