Burned in Connecticut, repaid in Ohio

Between 1777 and 1781 British raids burned or plundered nine Connecticut towns. In May 1792 the General Assembly repaid the losses with land instead of money: 500,000 acres at the western end of the state's Western Reserve, in what is now Erie and Huron counties, Ohio. Each person on the Assembly's list held a share in proportion to the loss set against their name.

The first map shows who was on the list, where, and for how much. The second shows where the claims landed in Ohio when the sections were drawn in 1808. The Ohio placements and amounts are read from the classification tables printed in an 1879 county history.

About this page

Reserve land never passed through a federal land office, so there are no General Land Office patents for it. On the main map Huron County has none at all and the rest of the Western Reserve is nearly blank. The Fire Lands are the western 500,000 acres of that blank.

The grantees were incorporated as "The Proprietors of the Half-Million Acres of Land Lying South of Lake Erie". The tract was surveyed into 120 quarter-township sections, the claims were grouped into 120 classifications of equal value, and on 9 November 1808 each classification drew one section by lot.

Who is on this list. The names are the people who lost property, or their heirs, as the Assembly recorded them in 1792. By the 1808 drawing many claims had been sold. The person on this list is the sufferer, not necessarily the person who took land in Ohio.

The Ohio map. The section assignments come from the classification tables printed township by township in Williams's 1879 history. Each table lists the original sufferers whose claims made up a section and who held those claims at the drawing. The tables were read from the page, and each section's amounts were added up against its printed total; the sections that do not add up are listed above the map. Where a printed figure was unreadable or plainly wrong, the figure that makes a split claim equal its 1792 loss was used. The holders at the drawing, mostly a few large buyers of claims, are not shown yet.

What is not finished. Names are as printed, with the source's abbreviations (Junr, Esqr, Wido, Nathl) and spellings. A few joint claims are still split over two rows, so the row count runs above the roughly 1,870 sufferers usually cited.

How it was read. The list was taken from the text of two separate scans of the printed volume and the two readings compared. An amount is marked when only one scan gave it, when the pence are missing, or when it has not been read yet. Farthings are approximate.
